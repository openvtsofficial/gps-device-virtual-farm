# Real-road simulation

The Windows x64 desktop app includes native Valhalla routing tools. The first preparation for a region downloads OpenStreetMap roads inside the app, builds a local graph, and saves a verified route catalog. No separate router, Docker, Python, Java, or setup command is required. The simulator uses saved route geometry while transmitting; it does not make routing requests during a run or reconnect.

## Prepare a region

1. Stop transmission. In **Settings**, choose **Real roads** and save the base latitude, longitude, and radius. An optional JSON array can define up to eight weighted regions; each needs `id`, `centerLatitude`, `centerLongitude`, `radiusKm`, and `fleetWeight`, with weights totaling 1.
2. On the main screen, press **Download and prepare roads**. The app chooses a [Geofabrik OSM extract](https://download.geofabrik.de/technical.html) covering the region plus a routing margin, or several smaller adjacent extracts whose indexed polygons cover it together. It downloads them over HTTPS and verifies their published checksums. Bundled Osmium tools clip and, when needed, merge the extracts before bundled Valhalla tools build the local road graph. The downloaded sources and graph stay in the app's user-data directory for later preparation. A new region requires internet and adequate free disk space.
3. The app proposes local streets and longer city and regional combinations, checks the Valhalla response against road geometry and reported road classes, and saves verified routes in immutable, checksummed packs. During preparation it reuses a bundled router on temporary loopback ports; if that service cannot start, the bundled command-line route engine remains available. It targets 2,000 distinct routes across the configured regions: at least 40% city routes with measured local-street distance, at least 15% suburban/regional routes of 25 km or more, and (for a radius of at least 50 km) at least 5% intercity routes of 80 km or more. Results depend on mapped drivable roads and accepted proposals.
4. A first build can stop at 500 or more verified routes once the saved packs support the configured fleet. These packs are selected automatically when one is available for every configured region. Start transmission from this partial library, then stop transmission and choose **Add more routes** to expand toward 2,000. Selecting the expanded pack starts fresh road-position assignments for the same IMEIs. The default diversity gate requires enough distinct closed tours for the moving fleet. The reduced-diversity setting permits less variety and destination trips that eventually park, subject to hard route capacity. The main-screen road status shows whether the selected packs can support the requested fleet.

The route-library administration panel is hidden from the normal interface. Preparation progress and cancellation appear beside the main-screen real-road status. The app caches downloaded extracts and graph data; previously selected packs remain on disk. There is no silent fallback to a public routing service.

## Progress and recovery

- The preparation display shows the current stage, a progress bar, routes saved toward the target, attempts used from the current budget, and an estimated time when enough measurements are available. The estimate can change as the app discovers how many proposed trips match real roads. Routes saved in a pack are durable; routes still being checked or staged may not yet be saved.
- The 500-route early-use point requires actual moving-device capacity, enough parked positions at least 50 m apart, and any enabled diversity gate for the configured fleet. A route count alone does not prove readiness. If a partial pack cannot support the current device count, add routes or lower the configured device count before starting.
- The app saves accepted routes after at most 100 new routes, after about 30 seconds of accepted work, and when a preparation action ends or is canceled. It remembers rejected combinations and the current planning round for the same region and road graph. Continue preparation to reuse downloaded map data and saved packs. Once a round runs out of untried proposals, the app explores a new, deterministic set of locations instead of repeating the old requests. Rebuilding the graph starts a new verification scope.
- Temporary failures from the bundled local router receive at most two additional attempts, with short waits between them. Each actual route request uses the action's attempt budget. Invalid waypoints, rejected geometry, and other deterministic failures are skipped rather than retried; public routing requests are never retried automatically. If the router remains unavailable, preparation pauses with the routes already saved.
- If 500 local attempts produce no new verified route, the app saves its place and tries a fresh planning round with different locations. After three consecutive rounds without a new route, preparation stops for that road graph and region. Check the chosen coordinates and radius; a changed region or rebuilt road graph starts a new search. Repeating the same action will not make another long series of ineffective requests. The 2,000-route target is a goal: sparse roads, the required city/regional mix, and the active geometry limit can prevent a region from reaching it.

## Coverage and limits

- The Windows x64 installer bundles Valhalla 3.9.1 and Osmium 1.19.1 native tools and their DLLs. Other platform builds do not currently include a road-graph builder, but can still play previously prepared route packs.
- Geofabrik has regional and country extracts; a border-spanning radius can require several sources. Each PBF is limited to 8 GiB, and multi-source graphs require matching source snapshots. If a smaller verified cover cannot be found, the app may still need a large parent extract and will report its URL and size when it exceeds the limit. Locations outside indexed coverage, very large regions, or insufficient disk/memory can still fail preparation. A 2,000-route target is a goal, not a guarantee for every area.
- Downloads use HTTPS from `download.geofabrik.de`, stay on that host through redirects, and are checked against Geofabrik's MD5 plus a locally saved SHA-256. App-generated route packs use SHA-256 geometry and manifest checksums.
- Road positions follow provider-validated path geometry. The app checks snap distance, route continuity, bounds, loop closure, and spatial novelty. Local route families must contain at least 20% residential, unclassified, or service-road distance. These checks improve route variety but do not prove every physical road or parking location is suitable for a real vehicle.
- Parked devices stay at least 50 metres apart in ground distance at both displayed and GT06-transmitted coordinate precision. The allocator uses verified city-road vertices and nearby positions, and relocates older checkpoints that violate the gap. Preparation and start checks report insufficient spaced stationary positions instead of placing vehicles too close together.
- Each IMEI keeps a stable route and distance cursor. Disconnects freeze progress. Only a provider-validated closed tour wraps to its beginning. Checkpoints are written every 15 seconds and on clean stop; a crash can roll back to the latest durable checkpoint.
- Route packs have a 2,000,000-point active-selection memory guard. This is not a measured fleet or map-size guarantee.
